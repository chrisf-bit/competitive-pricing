01 · The billboard effect

Search behaviour & market reach

Why being seen is the whole game.

Competitive priceYou enter the search
Higher visibilityYou rank on the page
More clicksTravellers land on you
More bookingsNet-new guests

How guests actually behave

The platform is a global search engine. Up to 90% of travellers discover a property here before they look anywhere else.

A high price loses the guest, not just the ranking. When you slide down the results, travellers rarely switch to your own site. They book a cheaper hotel on the same page.

Competitiveness reaches guests you cannot. It brings net-new international travellers you would never meet on your own, at no upfront cost.

Reward loyalty off the public rate. Use member-only deals or value-adds on your own site rather than holding public rates high, which hurts discovery everywhere.

Get the foundations right

Keep everything open and current. All room types available, with photos, amenities and services kept up to date.

Offer both rate types. Flexible and non-refundable rates, side by side.

Your non-refundable rate is the shop window. When travellers search with minimal filters it becomes your search price, lifting your ranking and bringing guests to your page.

The entry rate opens the door. Once guests land they can choose any rate you offer, but the competitive entry rate is what got them there.

Direct is not always cheaper

Add up global marketing reach and metasearch, then weigh it against direct acquisition and service costs. Commission is often the better deal, not the expensive one.

New guests become loyal guests. A traveller found here can turn into a repeat, direct booker. That is lasting, mutual value.

Turn a setup gap into a concrete win

Frame the opportunity around real money the partner is leaving on the table, for example recovering unsold room nights in high-demand windows.

A specific, measurable win is far easier to agree than an abstract call to "be more competitive".

03 · Win the right pockets of demand

Smart, targeted pricing

Not "cheaper for everyone".

Use fenced tools

There is no need to drop your baseline ADR for everyone.

Target the gap. Mobile Rates, Country Rates and long-stay deals capture specific underperforming demand without touching your headline price.

Fix the "setup gap"

Uncompetitive family pricing is usually unintentional: missing child rates or the wrong occupancy settings.

The system then prices children as adults, or books extra rooms. Correcting the setup solves it without touching the base rate.

04 · Protect brand value

Channels, wholesalers & competitor pressure

Keep pricing consistent and defensible.

Leaks & aggression

Wholesaler "leak". Private B2B rates sometimes resurface publicly and undercut the brand. The root cause is a supplier breaking the B2B agreement, not the platform.

"Same net for everyone". Matching blind competitor cuts confuses guests and erodes trust. Anchor on ROI and long-term brand value, and align your own base rates and promotions instead.

Programmes that help

Partner Offer. Helps travellers reach the best public price and keeps the listing visible when unauthorised B2B leaks appear.

Booking Sponsored Benefit (BSB). The platform funds part of the room cost for the guest. It is a win on three fronts:

Full price keptThe partner receives the price they set; commission is on the original price.
Guest pays lessA better price for the traveller drives conversion.
Lower riskPrepaid bookings mean cancellation risk is lower.
05 · Quick reframes

Handling data & credibility pushback

The objection, and how you answer it.

The objectionThe partner rejects our performance metrics because they conflict with their internal data.

Their PMS is their operational truth; platform data is a signal of market search intent. Even if the numbers differ slightly, the trend still shows demand going to competitors. Do a quick check (net versus gross, mapped rooms), then return to the opportunity.

The objectionThe partner rejects their peer group comparison as being inaccurate.

The peer group reflects how travellers search and compare, not who the owner sees as a rival. Holding a high rate today builds "visibility debt": fewer clicks now weaken performance later. Propose a small, controlled test.

The objectionThe partner inflates base rates to offset discounts.

An inflated base price is read as a fake discount and dampens visibility for travellers who are not signed in. Only a genuine discount grows real revenue, so revisit base rates and restrictions instead.

Diagnose before you advise

Probing questions

Let the partner do the talking.

Understand strategy & goals

What are you trying to achieve on Booking.com?
Can you walk me through how you set your current pricing?
How are you balancing priorities heading into the busy booking weeks?
Is this pricing gap a year-round strategy, or tied to a specific campaign?
How are you currently capturing family and international guests?

Challenge the direct-channel math

Are you already at 100% occupancy? If not, how are you filling the rest?
Have you checked the real stayed ADR on your website, and what your campaigns actually cost?
How much do you spend to acquire a new guest, especially international ones?
What share of your guests are repeaters, and what is their stayed ADR?
How do you track how many "direct" guests first found you on our platform?

Uncover bottlenecks & constraints

Have you seen more cancellations, or a shift in your channel mix?
When your price is higher here than on your own site, how do you avoid losing guests to the hotel next door?
Why are your larger rooms restricted from family searches?
Do you know where your wholesale rates end up, and which B2B partners resell them publicly?
I noticed non-refundable rates are switched off. Is there a reason?

Pitch a low-risk next step

What minimum occupancy would make a small test worth it for you?
Would you try a short pilot next month on a segment where you have no direct marketing?
Could we align public rates to restore ranking, while you reward direct guests with member-only deals?
Would you test this alignment for three weeks so we can measure the actual revenue lift?
Shall we set a minimum-stay rule on family rooms to protect margins, and trial it for a month?

Use the word "parity" in the conversation

Always: the partner is completely free to set their own pricing and distribution across every channel. In a no-parity market, ask only for their best available price and raise any gap reactively and neutrally. Keep the focus on how competitiveness affects their performance on the platform.

How to use this

Good to remember
